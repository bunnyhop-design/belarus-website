Places to see

Białowieża Forest

One of the last large stretches of Europe's ancient lowland forest, home to the European bison. A UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Nature

Lake Narach

The country's largest natural lake, roughly 80 km². A favourite spot for sailing and summer holidays.

Nature

Biarezina Reserve

Marshes, rivers and forest in the north, home to beavers, lynxes and elk.

Nature

Mir Castle

A 16th-century castle with towers and a moat in the town of Mir. A UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Nesvizh Castle

The residence of the Radziwiłł family, with a landscaped park and ponds. A UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Brest Fortress

A memorial complex on the border with Poland, a symbol of the 1941 defence.

Cities

Minsk

The modern capital, with wide avenues, the Trinity Suburb and the Upper Town.

Brest

The country's western gateway, home to the fortress and pedestrian Sovetskaya Street.

Grodno

A city on the Neman River with an Old and a New Castle and a cosy centre.

Vitebsk

The birthplace of Marc Chagall and host of the Slavianski Bazaar festival.

Polotsk

One of the oldest cities in the country, first mentioned in 862, home to the Cathedral of St. Sophia.

Gomel

A city in the southeast with the Rumyantsev-Paskevich Palace and park ensemble.

National cuisine

Draniki

Potato pancakes served with sour cream or machanka sauce.

Machanka

A thick meat-and-lard sauce served with pancakes or draniki.

Kholodnik

A cold beet soup made with kefir, popular in the summer heat.

Babka

A baked potato casserole with crispy pork cracklings.

Culture and traditions

A symbol of Belarusian culture is the rushnik — a linen towel with red woven ornament. Every diamond and cross in the pattern carries meaning: sun, earth, fertility.

Linen weaving and straw craft remain traditions passed down through generations.

Kupalle, celebrated on the night of July 7th, involves wreaths, bonfires and the search for the mythical fern flower.

Independence Day is marked on July 3rd, commemorating the liberation of Minsk in 1944. Dziady, a day of remembrance for ancestors, is another notable tradition.
